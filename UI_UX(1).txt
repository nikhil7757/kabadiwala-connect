Kabadiwala Connect: UI/UX Specification

Companion to PRD.md, TRD.md, BACKEND.md and APP_FLOW.md. This file
defines the look, feel and interaction rules for every screen. Follow it
exactly. Sections 1 to 14 set the foundation (legible, offline, light);
Section 15 is the aesthetic polish layer that makes the product look
sharp and memorable. Where something is not specified, choose the
simplest option that respects Section 11 (low-literacy rules) and record
it in DECISIONS.md.

1. Purpose and how to use the reference design

The team supplied two HTML files for a fitness studio website (brand
name “IRONFORGE”, truncated). They are inspiration only, for mood and
interaction ideas: dark cinematic contrast, one hot orange accent,
condensed uppercase display type, outlined headline text, monospace
micro-labels, corner-bracket frames, scan-line and grain textures,
blinking status dot, animated sound-bars, marquee ticker, flip cards,
sticky call-to-action bar, staggered reveal animations.

Hard rules for using the reference: 1. Do not copy its code, CSS blocks,
class names, HTML structure, copy text, brand name, images, or layout
order. Write everything fresh for this product. 2. Do not use any of its
external resources: no Tailwind CDN, no Google Fonts links, no Font
Awesome, no picsum or stock images. Everything must be bundled locally
so the app works offline. 3. Do not reproduce fitness content or
wording. After implementation, search the codebase for IRONFORGE, forge,
iron, picsum, cdn. and remove any hit. 4. Translate the ideas to this
product using the mapping in Section 2. Keep only ideas that help a
low-literacy collector working on a cheap phone in daylight.

2. Design idea: “Field Grade”

Concept: industrial, honest and legible, like a well-made weighing scale
label. Strong contrast, big shapes, one safety-orange accent for the
primary action, green for “recycled or confirmed”, and thin corner
brackets that frame the important things (the estimate, the QR code, the
lot record).

Two themes, one identity: - Field theme (collector app): light, warm
paper background, near-black ink, safety orange. Chosen because
collectors work outdoors in sunlight on entry-level LCD phones where
dark, low-contrast interfaces wash out. It keeps the reference’s bold
type and bracket motifs. - Control theme (recycler and admin): dark,
close to the reference mood (near-black, orange, silver), for desktop
and tablet use indoors.

label. - Status chips: neutral (surface-2), warning (warn-soft), accent
(accent-soft), success (success-soft), danger (danger-soft).

Scale for the Field theme (collector): body 18 px / 1.5; label 16 px;
small 14 px (rare); h2 24 px; h1 32 px / 1.1; amount display 56 px; big
step number 96 px outlined. Devanagari text: line-height at least 1.6
(avoids clipped vowel marks) and size one step larger than Latin at the
same role. Control theme (desktop): body 15 px, tables 14 px.

Outlined text effect: -webkit-text-stroke with transparent fill, only
for numerals and short English headings, never for Devanagari or body
text.

5. Layout, spacing and shape

-   Base unit 4 px. Spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48.
-   Collector baseline viewport 360 x 640; content max width 480 px
    centred on larger screens; screen padding 16 px; safe-area insets
    respected for the sticky bar.
-   Touch targets: 56 px height minimum for collector buttons and tiles
    (48 px absolute minimum for small chips).
-   Shape: squared and sturdy, radius 4 px on cards and buttons, 999 px
    on chips only. Field theme uses 2 px ink borders on primary cards
    and buttons; Control theme uses 1 px borders.
-   Corner brackets (“notch”): two L-shaped 2 px accent strokes, 14 px
    long, at top-left and bottom-right of a card. Only on featured
    elements (max two per screen).
-   Depth: no soft shadows on Field theme. Primary buttons use a hard 4
    px offset shadow in ink that collapses on press. Control theme uses
    borders and subtle surface steps.
-   Control layout: left sidebar 240 px (icons plus labels), top bar 56
    px, content max width 1280 px, tables with 44 px rows.

7. Motion

-   Easing: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). Durations: press 100 ms,
    fade 150 ms, page 200 ms, flip 500 ms, toast 300 ms.
-   Field theme: fade page transitions only; press effects; speaker
    bars; connectivity blink; one idle pulse on the New Lot tile.
-   Control theme: staggered list reveal (60 ms steps, max 6 items),
    ticker, hover states.
-   Respect prefers-reduced-motion: reduce: remove blink, pulse, ticker
    scrolling, flip animation and reveal; keep state changes instant.
-   Low-end guard: if navigator.deviceMemory is 2 or lower (when
    available), treat the device as reduced motion.
-   Forbidden on the collector app: backdrop blur, CSS filters on photos
    (except a simple brightness on disabled), parallax, video
    backgrounds, animated gradients, live SVG noise filters. The polish
    layer in Section 15 lists the only allowed motion and decoration
    additions.

8. Iconography and illustration

-   UI icons: lucide-react at 24 px (32 px in tiles), 2 px stroke.
-   Custom SVG pictograms (create as local SVG files, 64 x 64 viewBox, 2
    px ink stroke, flat, one orange accent shape each, no gradients, no
    text inside):
    -   Materials: CABLE (coiled cable), PCB (circuit board with chips),
        BATTERY (cell with plus sign), CRT (boxy old monitor), LCD (flat
        panel on stand), MOTOR (cylinder with shaft and magnet arc),
        PLASTIC (crushed bottle and casing), OTHER (open box).
    -   Safety: no-burning (flame with cross), do-not-open-CRT (monitor
        with hammer crossed), safe-battery (battery with caution
        triangle), no-acid (droplet on board with cross), wear-gloves
        (hand with glove), keep-children-away (small figure with cross).
    -   Status: tick in circle (green), cross in circle (red), clock
        (waiting), cloud-off (offline).
-   Illustrations: only inline SVG shapes generated in code or files; no
    photographs except the user’s own lot photos; no stock images.
-   App icon: orange square with a simple bracket-framed recycling arrow
    mark (create as SVG, export 192 and 512 PNG plus maskable).

11. Accessibility and low-literacy rules

1.  Every screen has one clear primary action; never more than two
    visible buttons.
2.  Every meaningful text has an icon; every critical screen has a
    speaker button.
3.  Avoid typing: use number pad, chips and icon grids. Free text only
    for recycler and admin forms.
4.  Short sentences (under 8 words where possible). No jargon: use
    “buyer” (खरीदार) not “counterparty”; use “code” not “OTP” in UI
    copy.
5.  Touch targets at least 56 px (48 px minimum); spacing between
    targets at least 8 px.
6.  Contrast and colour-independence per Section 3.
7.  Error messages: pictogram plus one line plus audio plus the fix (“No
    internet. Your work is saved. Try again later.”).
8.  Confirmation for destructive actions with green tick and red cross
    buttons, both with icons and audio.
9.  Text size setting (Normal 100 percent, Large 125 percent) applied
    through a root font-size variable.
10. Screen reader labels in the current language; focus order follows
    visual order; visible focus ring using --kc-focus, 3 px.
11. Do not rely on hover, drag or long press as the only way to do
    something. (Photo delete uses a visible delete icon; long press is
    optional shortcut only.)
12. Language switching never loses screen state.
13. Money format: rupee symbol plus digits with Indian grouping (for
    example 12,500); weights in kg with one decimal.
14. Sunlight legibility: keep text on flat colours, never on photos or
    gradients.

13. Performance rules for the UI

Use SVG for pictograms; lazy load recycler and admin routes and
recharts; import only used font subsets; no image larger than 300 KB
shipped in the bundle; model and audio load on demand and cache; avoid
re-renders in the number pad and slider; virtualise lists only if a list
exceeds 100 rows.

14. Implementation checklist for the AI model

1.  Read Section 1 and 2; write a short paragraph in DECISIONS.md
    stating which reference ideas you used and confirming nothing was
    copied.
2.  Create tokens (Section 3), fonts (Section 4) and the two themes
    first, with a small /dev/ui-kit page (development only) that shows
    every component in both themes.
3.  Build components from Section 6, then screens from Sections 9 and
    10.
4.  Verify: no external requests (network blocked test), contrast pairs
    recorded, touch targets at least 48 px, Devanagari renders without
    clipped marks, reduced-motion works, Large text setting works, all
    i18n keys exist in hi, mr and en.
5.  Search for forbidden strings from Section 1 rule 3 and remove them.
6.  Test on a real entry-level Android phone in bright light and with
    airplane mode on.
7.  Build the Section 15 components (ScaleLabel, GaugeDial, TickDraw,
    HazardTape, RecycleLoader, ChainViewer, RouteLine) alongside the
    other components, add lib/motion.ts, and show each one in
    /dev/ui-kit in both themes with motion on and off.
8.  Run the visual QA checklist in Section 15.8 and record the result in
    DECISIONS.md.

15. Aesthetic layer: make it look sharp and memorable

Sections 1 to 14 keep the app legible and light on a cheap phone. This
section is the polish layer that makes it feel designed rather than
default. Everything here is built from inline SVG, CSS transforms and
opacity, so it stays offline and cheap. Where it conflicts with the “not
used” and “forbidden” lists in Sections 2 and 7, this section lists the
exact exceptions. Build the components early (Phases 6 to 9) and tune
them in Phase 10.

15.1 Look in one sentence

A well-made weighing-scale label meets hazard tape: warm paper, hard ink
lines, one safety-orange accent, bracket corners, big numerals that feel
printed, and small moments of motion when something succeeds.

15.4 Motion budget and kill switch

-   Animate only transform, opacity and SVG stroke-dashoffset. Never
    animate width, height, top, left, box-shadow or filters.
-   At most two animations running at once. Each 500 ms or less, except
    the 800 ms splash.
-   One helper decides everything:

    // lib/motion.ts
    export function motionOK(): boolean {
      const reduced = matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches;
      const mem = (navigator as { deviceMemory?: number }).deviceMemory;
      const saveData = (navigator as { connection?: { saveData?: boolean } }).connection?.saveData === true;
      return !(reduced || (mem !== undefined && mem <= 2) || saveData);
    }
    // on boot: document.documentElement.dataset.motion = motionOK() ? 'on' : 'off';

    [data-motion="off"] *, [data-motion="off"] *::before, [data-motion="off"] *::after {
      animation: none !important;
      transition: none !important;
    }

State changes must still be visible with motion off (tick shown, amount
shown, needle in place).

Rules: the gradient strips above are decorative and static. Text is
never placed on them, so the sunlight-legibility rule in Section 11
still holds. Contrast pairs for anything new are checked and recorded
like the others.

15.6 Exceptions to earlier “not used” lists

1.  Backdrop blur is allowed only in the Control theme at widths of 1024
    px and above, on the top bar and drawers, with a solid surface
    fallback under @supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px)). Still
    forbidden in the collector app.
2.  Static gradients are allowed for the hazard strip, perforation, and
    the Control login spotlight. Animated gradients stay forbidden.
3.  SVG stroke-dashoffset animation is allowed for the tick, splash mark
    and scan corners. Live SVG noise filters stay forbidden.

15.7 Microcopy tone

Short, warm, direct. Success lines: “Saved on your phone”, “Buyer
chosen”, “Handover done”. Errors say what happened and what to do, never
blame the user. Hindi and Marathi lines use everyday spoken words (भाव,
माल), not formal or English loan words when a common local word exists.

15.8 Visual QA checklist

1.  Screenshots at 360 x 640 (Field) and 1280 x 800 (Control) of every
    screen in Sections 9 and 10, in Hindi, Marathi and English; compare
    against this section.
2.  Large text setting (125 percent) keeps every screen usable without
    clipped text or overlapped buttons.
3.  Motion off (reduced-motion setting and low-memory simulation): all
    information is still visible and nothing is stuck mid-animation.
4.  Sunlight check on a real phone at full brightness: amounts, buttons
    and the QR are readable without shading the screen.
5.  No text sits on a photo, a gradient strip or the dot grid.
6.  Frame rate stays smooth while scrolling the lots list on a low-end
    phone or with 4x CPU throttle in dev tools.
